Mercedes-Benz Online Finance Journey

A Digital Customer Experience proposal created for a Mercedes-Benz Financial Services interview exercise.

Portfolio Note: ⚠️ This is a speculative case study created in response to a Mercedes-Benz Financial Services interview task. The work was not commissioned, implemented, or endorsed by Mercedes-Benz.

📁 1. Process Overview

Process Index: Background | Discovery | Opportunity Areas | Validation | Success Metrics | Artifacts | Reflection | Key Learnings

📁 2. Background

Mercedes-Benz Financial Services identified friction within its online showroom finance experience. Customers struggled to understand finance terminology, compare options confidently, and determine which plan suited their needs. As a result, some users abandoned the journey before requesting a finance quote.

Rather than jumping directly into interface changes, I approached the challenge as a Digital Customer Experience problem. My goal was to understand where confidence broke down during the journey and identify improvements that could reduce hesitation while supporting informed decision-making.

Core Problem: Customers were making one of their biggest purchasing decisions while navigating unfamiliar terminology and limited guidance.

📁 3. Discovery Strategy

The interview brief asked how I would understand the customer's challenges before proposing solutions. Instead of relying on assumptions, I structured my approach around three complementary sources of insight.

📊 Website Analytics

The first step would be reviewing behavioural data to identify where confidence dropped during the finance journey.

  • Finance page exit rates
  • Quote completion rates
  • Calculator abandonment
  • Navigation flow between pricing pages

These metrics would reveal where customers hesitated before committing to the next step.

💬 Customer Support Insights

Behavioural analytics explains where people struggle, but support conversations explain why.

  • Live chat transcripts
  • Customer service emails
  • Call centre questions

Repeated questions often reveal opportunities to simplify content before customers need assistance.

👥 Usability Sessions

I proposed running a small set of moderated usability sessions with realistic tasks.

For example:

"Choose the finance option that best fits a £350 monthly budget."

Watching participants complete realistic scenarios would uncover hesitation that analytics alone cannot explain.

Finance Calculator

What this represents: An interactive point in the journey where customers adjust deposits, durations, and mileage to estimate monthly payments.

Purpose: This helps illustrate how users engage with key financial inputs before highlighting areas of behavioural friction.

Key Insight: The strongest customer experiences are built by combining quantitative behaviour with qualitative understanding rather than relying on either source in isolation.

📁 4. Opportunity Areas

After framing the problem through research, I focused on identifying improvements that balanced customer value with implementation effort. Rather than redesigning the entire journey, I proposed reducing friction at the moments where customers experienced the most uncertainty.

Design Principle: Remove uncertainty before asking customers to make financial decisions.

💡 Opportunity 1: Contextual Finance Tooltips

Customers often encounter unfamiliar terms such as PCP, APR, and Hire Purchase. Instead of forcing users to leave the page to search for definitions, I proposed explaining these terms at the moment they're needed.

  • Hover or tap to expand explanations.
  • Plain-English definitions.
  • No disruption to the purchase journey.

This approach reduces cognitive load while allowing customers to continue exploring without losing context.

Concept Visualisation: Finance Tooltip

What this concept illustrates: A proposed tooltip interaction that gives customers clear, immediate explanations of finance terms like PCP without leaving the page.

Purpose: This demonstrates how contextual guidance can reduce confusion and support quicker, more confident decision‑making.

📊 Opportunity 2: Side by Side Comparison Cards

One of the biggest barriers to choosing finance is comparing multiple options mentally. Instead of reading long paragraphs, customers should be able to compare key differences instantly.

Comparison Customer Benefit
Monthly Cost Easier budgeting
Ownership Clear expectations
Mileage Better suitability
Flexibility Faster decision-making
Concept Visualisation: Comparison Cards

What this visual represents: A structured comparison of PCP, Hire Purchase, and Leasing, showing how clearer side by side information can support more confident decision‑making.

Purpose: This becomes one of the strongest visuals in the case study by illustrating how simplified comparisons reduce cognitive load for customers.

🧭 Opportunity 3: "Help Me Choose" Decision Wizard

Choosing between finance products can feel overwhelming. Instead of asking customers to understand every product upfront, I proposed a guided decision experience that recommends suitable options based on their priorities.

Rather than replacing product information, the wizard acts as a confidence-building tool before comparison.

Simple Three-Step Flow
  1. Choose your monthly budget.
  2. Estimate annual mileage.
  3. Decide whether ownership matters most.

At the end of the journey, customers receive a recommended finance option alongside an explanation of why it fits their answers.

Concept Visualisation: Decision Wizard

What this concept demonstrates: A guided three‑step decision flow that helps customers clarify their budget, mileage, and ownership preferences before receiving a tailored finance recommendation.

Purpose: This visual shows how a simplified wizard can reduce decision fatigue and support clearer, more confident finance choices.

📁 5. Prioritising Improvements

Not every improvement needs to launch immediately. A key part of Digital CX is balancing customer impact with delivery effort, so I grouped recommendations into quick wins and longer-term investments.

Prioritisation Principle: Deliver confidence-building improvements early while planning larger product enhancements for future iterations.

⚡ Quick Wins

  • Hover tooltips
  • Plain-English content updates
  • Clearer finance labels
  • Improved content hierarchy

Lower implementation effort with immediate customer value.

🚀 Strategic Projects

  • Comparison cards
  • Decision wizard
  • Personalised recommendations
  • Future account experiences

Higher investment but stronger long-term CX improvements.

📁 7. Validation Strategy

The interview brief also asked how I would verify whether these ideas actually improved the customer experience. Rather than moving straight into development, I proposed validating concepts through lightweight testing before investing in implementation.

Validation Principle: Reduce delivery risk by testing customer understanding before writing production code.

📝 Prototype Before Build

Although I didn't create prototypes during the interview exercise, my proposed validation approach would begin with low-fidelity wireframes before progressing to interactive Figma concepts.

  • Sketch core layouts.
  • Create clickable prototypes.
  • Test realistic customer tasks.
  • Iterate before development.

This allows teams to identify usability issues while changes remain inexpensive.

👥 Moderated Usability Testing

I proposed observing customers completing realistic scenarios instead of asking whether they "liked" the design.

Example task: "Find the finance option that best matches your monthly budget."

Watching hesitation, questions, and decision-making often reveals friction that surveys alone cannot capture.

⚖️ A/B Testing

Once validated concepts showed promise, I would compare the new experience against the existing journey using A/B testing.

  • Current finance journey (Control)
  • Improved finance journey (Variant)

This provides measurable evidence before rolling out changes to all customers.

Concept Visualisation: Validation Workflow

What this workflow outlines: A simple validation cycle showing how prototypes are tested, refined, and prepared for launch through iterative feedback.

Purpose: This mirrors the approach discussed during the interview while remaining accurate to what was proposed rather than fully executed.

📁 8. Measuring Success

A successful customer experience should be measurable. Rather than relying on opinion alone, I proposed tracking both behavioural outcomes and customer confidence after launch.

Success isn't only fewer clicks, it's greater confidence when making a financial decision.

📉 Finance Page Exits

A reduction in exits would suggest customers better understand the information they're seeing.

📈 Quote Completion

More completed finance quotes indicate increased confidence and reduced friction.

☎️ Support Requests

Fewer questions about finance terminology suggest the interface explains concepts more effectively.

⭐ Customer Confidence

Short feedback surveys can measure whether customers feel more confident choosing a finance option.

📁 10. Reflection

This project reinforced that Digital Customer Experience is about reducing uncertainty, not simply redesigning interfaces. When customers make high-value financial decisions, clarity becomes just as important as aesthetics.

Looking back, I'm pleased that my approach prioritised customer understanding before proposing solutions. Rather than redesigning the entire experience, I focused on reducing friction at the moments where confidence was most likely to break down.

If I Expanded This Project Today

  • Create interactive Figma prototypes.
  • Run moderated usability testing with prospective customers.
  • Validate finance terminology with real users.
  • Test personalised recommendations through experimentation.
  • Build a measurable roadmap tied to conversion and satisfaction metrics.
Growth Mindset: One of the biggest lessons from this project was recognising that strong customer experiences emerge through continuous testing and iteration rather than a single redesign.

📁 11. Key Learnings

What I Learned: Clear communication, thoughtful prioritisation, and measurable outcomes are just as valuable as interface design when improving customer experiences.
  • Discovery should always come before solution design.
  • Plain language reduces cognitive load during financial decisions.
  • Small UX improvements can influence conversion behaviour.
  • Customer confidence should be treated as a measurable product outcome.
  • Testing concepts before development reduces implementation risk.
  • Product thinking involves balancing customer value with delivery effort.

📁 12. Final Thoughts

Although this began as a speculative interview exercise, rebuilding it as a portfolio case study allowed me to reflect on how I approach Digital Customer Experience problems more strategically today.

The exercise demonstrates how I think through customer research, prioritisation, validation, and measurement skills that extend beyond interface design into broader product thinking.

Takeaway: The strongest digital experiences don't remove every decision, they make important decisions feel easier.
