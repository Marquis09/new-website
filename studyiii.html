MMU TV Website: Web Development & UX

A creative platform for the MMU community to showcase and explore media projects

University project · 3 weeks · View live site

Executive Summary

  • Situation: MMU students and staff needed an easy way to share and explore creative video projects.
  • Task: Design and build a mobile-friendly site from scratch that suits both creators and casual viewers.
  • Action: Built a responsive Bootstrap site with a clean homepage, simple search and a short sign-up flow.
  • Result: A working responsive site, delivered in three weeks.
  • Status: University project. User testing and an accessibility audit have not been done.

Project Details

  • My Role: Web Developer UX Designer
  • Duration: 3 weeks
  • Tools: HTML5 CSS3 Bootstrap Figma
  • Project Type: University project
Process Index: 1. Overview | 2. Challenges | 3. Decisions | 4. Solutions | 5. Evidence | 6. Reflection
📁 1. Project Overview

MMU TV is a website for showcasing creative projects from Manchester Metropolitan University students and staff. The goal was to make it easy for people to explore videos, sign up and engage with the MMU community. It was a university project built over three weeks.

The challenge: design and build a mobile-friendly site from scratch that could host varied video content and stay intuitive for both student creators and casual viewers.
Visit the Live MMU TV Website
📁 2. Challenges & Design Goals

Key challenges

  • Layout: no clear, modern way to show varied video content.
  • Mobile: it had to work well on phones, with accessibility in mind.
  • Search: a simple way to filter videos.
  • Audience: an experience that suits both students and staff.

Design objectives

  • Responsive: mobile-first for all devices.
  • Navigation: find and watch videos quickly.
  • Visual appeal: engaging sections for creative work.
  • Simplicity: a very simple sign-up and interaction flow.
📁 3. Key Decisions & Alternatives

The site is built with HTML5, CSS3 and Bootstrap, with Figma used for design. These were the main choices.

DecisionAlternative consideredWhy I chose this
Bootstrap for the layoutWriting all the CSS from scratchThree weeks was short. Bootstrap’s responsive grid gave reliable mobile behaviour and left more time for design decisions.
Mobile-first layoutDesktop first, adapted laterMany viewers watch media on their phones, so the small screen came first.
One simple search featureAdvanced filters and categoriesA simple search was achievable in the time and covers the main need: finding a video.
Short sign-up flowA fuller profile at registrationFewer fields lower the barrier for creators and viewers joining the community.

📁 5. Feedback & Evidence

This was a short university project, and the evidence is what I built, not measured results.

Completed

  • Responsive site with homepage, search and sign-up
  • Design and build within the three-week timeframe

Not yet done

  • Usability testing with students or staff
  • An accessibility audit (the site aims for accessibility but has not been checked against a standard)
  • Testing search with a large amount of content
📁 6. Reflection & Next Steps

Building MMU TV helped me bridge front-end development and UX thinking. I learned to design for different audiences, keep interactions simple when people are exploring media, and balance creativity with usability.

What I learned:

  • Clear navigation helps people explore content with confidence.
  • Mobile-first layouts matter for media platforms.
  • Simple features are easier to finish well in a short project.
  • Consistent visual design makes a site feel coherent.

What I would do next:

  • Test the site with a few students and staff and record the results.
  • Run an accessibility check against WCAG 2.1 AA.
  • Add categories and filters to the search once there is more content.
