Project Coward: Interactive ARG Interface

From wholesome community portal to glitch-driven psychological horror

Univercity Collaborative team project (CoLabs 4 campaign) · Team of 6 · View live hub

Executive Summary

  • Situation: A collaborative ARG needed a web hub that could shift from a cosy community space to a corrupted one as the story unfolded.
  • Task: As lead web and UI developer, design and build the dual-state interface and connect it to the campaign’s external platforms.
  • Action: Built the hub with a login gate, a record trigger and glitch effects, and re-routed traffic when a platform restricted the campaign.
  • Result: The campaign launched on schedule with a working hub. Evidence of player behaviour is informal.
  • Status: Launched. Structured playtesting and an accessibility check of the effects have not been done.

Project Details

  • My Role: Lead Web & UI Developer
  • Team Size: 6 (engineering, visual arts, narrative, PR)
  • Duration: CoLabs 4 campaign launch
  • Stack: HTML5 CSS3 JavaScript (ES6+)
Process Index: 1. Background | 2. My Contribution | 3. Engineering | 4. Before/After | 5. Prototype | 6. Testing | 7. Challenge | 8. Reflection
📁 1. Background & Vision

Project Coward began as a collaborative Alternate Reality Game (ARG) exploring digital personas, cyberbullying and psychological horror through a fictional Vtuber influencer. The hub, the VTuber Nexus, was the players’ entry point: a wholesome, inviting space that gradually degrades into a corrupted, glitch-filled one, mirroring the character’s emotional deterioration.

My role was to design and engineer this dual-state interface so that every visual shift, glitch effect and narrative beat matched the character’s arc. The site needed to feel alive, respond to player actions and connect to external platforms.

The early interface was wholesome to represent Stella’s innocence and approachability. The later state reflected her unravelling under cyberbullying pressure. Content note: the project deals with online hate and mental health in a fictional, horror setting.

📁 3. Interactive Engineering: Dual-State Architecture

The hub was built around two states, a stable wholesome environment and a corrupted horror-driven one, with transitions triggered by player actions.

  • The portal gate: an in-fiction login with credentials (admin / password123) found through narrative clues. It is a story device, not real security.
  • The record trigger: a UI interaction that starts the glitch effects and marks the move into the horror phase.
  • Dynamic theme degradation: DOM overrides, corrupted image swaps and altered merchandise pricing to suggest psychological collapse.

📁 6. Testing & Evidence

Informal testing suggested that players explored the interactive elements and found the hidden triggers without being told where to look. This was observation, not measured data, so it supports the approach without proving it.

Completed

  • Informal testing of the login, trigger and glitch sequence during the campaign
  • Live launch with the hub at the centre of the campaign

Not yet done

  • Structured playtests with recorded results
  • Measuring how many players found the portal and trigger
  • Checking flashing and motion effects against accessibility guidance (including reduced-motion support)
  • Analytics on cross-platform referrals
📁 7. Technical Challenge & Adaptability

Twitter’s safety systems shadowbanned our automated character feed because of its high-volume fictional activity, which threatened the ARG’s cross-platform progression.

Action

I re-engineered the routing map to send more traffic to the active Reddit and Instagram channels, and added fallback handling and string replacement in the front end so the story kept flowing when players hit restricted or broken links.

Result

Players kept moving between the live platforms, dead links were kept to a minimum and the campaign launched on schedule with a stable hub.

📁 8. Reflection, Limitations & Next Steps

Adapting in real time, from a planned edited final video to a live-stream reveal, showed me how important flexibility is in collaborative media. The live stream kept the core narrative and added a sense of unpredictability.

The website, glitch effects and live stream worked together to convey themes of online hate, mental health and digital personas. Working with the team across research on Vtubers and cyberbullying, environment design and AI in games taught me to bring different disciplines into one interactive experience.

Limitations:

  • Evidence of player behaviour is informal and was not recorded.
  • Glitch effects were not checked against accessibility guidance for flashing or motion.
  • The login is deliberately simple and is not secure.

What I would do next:

  • Run structured playtests and record how players find the triggers.
  • Add a reduced-motion option and check flashing against accessibility guidance.
  • Track referrals between platforms to see where players drop off.
