New City Church MK

From abandoned Wix site to a modern, welcoming digital home

Real volunteer project for a local church · Sole designer and developer · Built with permission to share as a portfolio reference

Executive Summary

  • Situation: The church's Wix site had been left behind after the original developers departed. No one remaining could update content or maintain it.
  • Task: Rebuild the site as the sole designer and developer, so a nervous first-time visitor could find what they need and church staff could publish news without developer help.
  • Action: Audited the old site, spoke informally with church contacts, simplified the navigation around a "Plan Your Visit" path, and built a mobile-first static front-end with a simple CMS for news.
  • Result: The site is live in preview and complete from the design and development side. It has been reviewed by the pastor and several church members, and only small changes were requested, all of which I made.
  • Status: Awaiting church-side items before launch: a new welcome video, replacement photos, and the domain so the site can move to the church's own hosting.

Project Details

  • My Role: UX Designer UI Designer Front-end Developer
  • Tools & Tech: HTML5 CSS3 JavaScript Netlify / Decap CMS GitHub Pages
  • Project Type: Real volunteer project for a local Independent Evangelical church (FIEC). Not university coursework.
  • Constraints: Informal brief · Sole designer and developer · Must be maintainable by non-technical staff · Preserve the warm, informal church identity
  • Timeline: 2025 – 2026
Process Index: 1. Brief | 2. Problem | 3. Process | 4. Personas | 5. IA | 6. Design | 7. Decisions | 8. Wireframes | 9. Solution | 10. Feedback | 11. Tech | 12. Reflection

📁 1. The Brief & Opportunity

The project began with a simple, human request. The pastor’s wife approached me and asked if I would take a look at the church website. The original site had been built in Wix by an external team who had since left. No one remaining in the church had access, training, or the ability to update content. The site was effectively abandoned: still live, still representing the church online, but frozen in time and increasingly out of step with how the community actually felt.

This was not a formal agency brief. It was a real need from a real community: a digital front door that felt welcoming, that newcomers could trust, and that the church itself could own and maintain without needing external developers for every small change.

Core Challenge: Rebuild the entire web presence from the ground up so that it felt contemporary, mobile-first, accessible, and easy for non-technical staff to update, while preserving the warm, informal, Bible-centred identity of New City Church MK.

📁 2. Understanding the Problem

I began by auditing the existing Wix site and speaking informally with people connected to the church about what they needed from the website. This was conversation-based discovery rather than formal user research.

Issues with the legacy Wix site:

  • Ownership gap: Original developers had left. No one in the church could edit content, fix broken elements, or update news.
  • Performance & weight: Typical of many Wix builds: heavy, slower on mobile, multiple third-party scripts.
  • Limited newcomer guidance: Critical practical questions (parking, dress code, children’s provision, what a Sunday feels like) were hard to find or missing.
  • Navigation & hierarchy: Structure did not prioritise the primary job of a church website: helping a first-time visitor feel confident enough to come.
  • Visual language: Full-bleed photography with heavy overlays felt dated and did not scale well across devices.
  • Maintainability: Locked into a platform the church could no longer control.
Success needed to look like: a site that a nervous first-time visitor could understand in under 30 seconds, that a parent could check for kids provision on their phone, that a regular could quickly find the latest sermon, and that a church admin could update without calling a developer.

📁 3. My Role & Process

I worked as the sole designer and developer, volunteering my time. I owned the full path from discovery through to front-end implementation, CMS setup and review with the church.

  • 1. Discovery: Site audit of the Wix original, informal conversations with church contacts, a look at other FIEC and independent church sites, and a content inventory.
  • 2. Information Architecture: Defined primary user jobs, reduced navigation to the essentials, prioritised the newcomer journey, mapped content to pages.
  • 3. Wireframing: Low-fidelity layouts for Home, Plan Your Visit, Sermons, Church Life and supporting pages (desktop and mobile).
  • 4. Visual Design: Established the colour system (deep magenta brand), typography (Plus Jakarta Sans), component patterns and photography direction.
  • 5. Development: Semantic HTML5, modern CSS (Flexbox/Grid), responsive breakpoints, performance-conscious asset handling and accessibility foundations.
  • 6. CMS & Integrations: Netlify / Decap CMS for news, plus embeds for ChurchSuite, the church calendar, YouTube and Spotify, and an online giving page.
  • 7. Review with the church: Shared the live preview with the pastor and church members, collected feedback, and made the requested changes.

📁 4. Working Personas

Church websites serve a mixed audience: curious first-timers, regulars looking for practical info, parents, and staff. These three working personas were drawn from my informal conversations with church contacts and my audit of the old site. They are design tools rather than the output of formal research, and they kept decisions focused on real needs.

Persona 1: Sarah, the Curious Newcomer

Recently moved to Milton Keynes or exploring faith. Primary questions: What time do you meet? Where do I park? What should I wear? Is there something for my children? Will anyone talk to me?

Design response: A prominent “Plan Your Visit” path, clear service-time card, dress-code reassurance, kids information, and an obvious contact route.

Persona 2: David, the Regular / Parent

Already connected or considering a group. Needs fast access to sermons, group times and news.

Design response: Persistent, short navigation to Sermons and Church Life, scannable group lists, and a living news section.

Persona 3: Church Staff / Admin

Needs to publish news and announcements without developer help.

Design response: A simple CMS for news posts so the front-end stays mostly static and maintainable.

📁 5. Information Architecture

I deliberately kept the primary navigation short. The website’s main job is not to showcase every ministry in equal weight; it is to help a first-time visitor feel confident enough to walk through the door on Sunday.

Primary navigation: Home · About Us · Sermons · Church Life · News  +  the high-visibility “Plan Your Visit” CTA.

Supporting content (Giving, Privacy, Data Protection, Safeguarding, Contact) lives in the footer and secondary paths. News is CMS-powered so the church can keep the site alive without touching code.

📁 6. Design System & Visual Language

The visual system needed to feel warm and contemporary without losing the approachable, non-corporate character of the church. I chose a deep magenta as the primary accent, energetic yet serious, paired with clean slate neutrals and soft surfaces.

Colour palette & type sample
Primary magenta, neutrals, and Plus Jakarta Sans in use.

Visual direction
Four pillars that guided every design decision.

Key design choices:

  • Typography: Plus Jakarta Sans for a modern, highly legible geometric sans that works well at both display and body sizes.
  • Cards & radius: Soft 14–28 px rounded cards with subtle shadows to create hierarchy without harsh boxes.
  • Photography: Real community imagery (families, children, gatherings) rather than generic stock or heavy overlays.
  • CTAs: Primary actions always in solid magenta; secondary actions as text links or ghost buttons to protect hierarchy.
  • Mobile-first: Large touch targets, a pull-up footer drawer on mobile, and no horizontal overflow at common viewport sizes.

📁 7. Key Decisions & Alternatives

A few choices shaped the project more than any others. Each one was a trade-off between what looks best, what the church needs, and what the church can realistically maintain.

Decision Alternative considered Why I chose this
Custom static front-end Stay on Wix, or move to another site builder The church had already been left stranded by a platform it couldn’t control. Plain HTML/CSS is portable, fast, and readable by any future developer.
Decap CMS for news only A full CMS for every page News is the one thing staff genuinely need to change regularly. Keeping everything else static keeps the site simple and hard to break.
Short navigation + “Plan Your Visit” A full menu listing every ministry The site’s main job is to give newcomers confidence. Fewer choices makes the first step obvious.
Embed ChurchSuite, calendar, YouTube and Spotify Rebuild those features from scratch The church already uses these tools. Embedding them avoids duplicate admin work and keeps information up to date in one place.
Real community photography Stock images or full-bleed overlays Stock imagery undermines trust. Real families and gatherings show newcomers who they will actually meet.

📁 9. The Solution

The new site is a custom-built, semantic front-end with a lightweight CMS for news. It prioritises the newcomer journey while remaining useful for regulars and maintainable by staff.

Key features delivered:

  • Clear newcomer path: A dedicated “Plan Your Visit” page answering the questions people actually ask before attending for the first time.
  • Service times & location front and centre: Visible on the Home hero with a directions link.
  • Sermons hub: Scripture-led presentation with easy links out to Logos and Spotify for ongoing listening, plus embedded YouTube.
  • Church Life overview: Homegroups, youth, kids and other groups presented scannably.
  • Church tools built in: ChurchSuite and the church calendar embedded, and an online giving page for donations.
  • News CMS: Staff can publish announcements without touching code.
  • Mobile footer drawer: A pull-up bar at the bottom of the page gives quick access to contact details and links.
  • Performance & accessibility foundations: Clean HTML/CSS, responsive layout, readable text sizes and keyboard-friendly navigation.

📁 10. Feedback & Evidence

I shared the live preview with the pastor and a small number of church members and asked for their reactions. This was informal feedback, not a formal usability study, and I have kept the two clearly separate below.

Completed

  • Audit of the legacy Wix site
  • Informal conversations with church contacts
  • Review of the live preview by the pastor and several church members
  • Small changes requested during review, all implemented

Not yet done

  • Formal usability testing with first-time visitors
  • Full accessibility audit
  • Before/after performance measurement
  • Post-launch review of how newcomers use the site

What a church member said after trying the preview:

“I think it’s really good. I like the drag up bar at the bottom, that’s a great feature. I don’t think I would change anything other than needing an updated video to show the correct school.”
Church member, informal feedback

Two things stand out. The pull-up bar was noticed and liked without being pointed out, which suggests it is discoverable. And the only change this tester wanted was content (a video), not design or structure. With a handful of reviewers this is encouraging rather than conclusive, which is why formal testing sits on the next-steps list.

📁 11. Technical Approach

I chose a deliberately simple, durable stack so the church would not be dependent on a single external developer or proprietary platform again.

  • Markup: Semantic HTML5: readable structure, strong accessibility baseline, easy for future developers to understand.
  • Styling: Modern CSS (Flexbox / Grid, custom properties): no heavy framework dependency, full control over performance and design tokens.
  • Typography: Plus Jakarta Sans via Google Fonts: contemporary geometric sans with excellent screen readability.
  • Icons: Phosphor Icons: lightweight, consistent, accessible icon set.
  • CMS: Netlify CMS / Decap CMS for news: Git-based, free, allows non-technical editors to publish without code access.
  • Embeds: ChurchSuite, calendar, YouTube and Spotify, so the church keeps managing content in the tools it already uses.
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages for the preview. The plan is to move it to the church’s own GitHub account with the CMS running once the domain is supplied.

📁 12. Status, Reflection & Next Steps

Current status:

From the design and development side, the site is complete and viewable at the live link. It is not yet launched on the church’s domain because the remaining items depend on the church:

  • Recording and supplying a new welcome video so I can embed it (the current one shows the wrong school).
  • Supplying replacement photos for the site.
  • Providing the domain so the site can move to the church’s own GitHub and the CMS can go live.

What this project demonstrates:

  • Turning an informal, real-world request into a structured UX process.
  • End-to-end ownership: discovery, IA, wireframes, visual design, front-end code, CMS and client review.
  • User-centred prioritisation (newcomer confidence as the primary success measure).
  • Practical technical decisions that protect the client’s long-term independence.
  • Working with a non-technical client, gathering feedback and acting on it.

Limitations and what I would do next:

  • Feedback came from a small, friendly group, so it validates direction but isn’t a substitute for testing with real first-time visitors.
  • I would run short usability sessions on the Plan Your Visit page with two or three newcomers.
  • I would run a formal accessibility check (contrast, keyboard and screen-reader testing) and record before/after performance figures.
  • I would hold a short handover session so staff are confident publishing news through the CMS.
This project reinforced that good design for community organisations is less about visual novelty and more about reducing anxiety and increasing clarity. A first-time visitor to a church often arrives with quiet questions. The website’s job is to answer those questions before they even leave the house. Building something the church can actually own and update has been just as important as making it look contemporary.
